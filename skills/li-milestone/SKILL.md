# li-milestone

Milestone posts get more reach than almost anything else a person posts,
because their network wants to congratulate them. Most waste it on "I'm
happy to share that I'm starting a new position as..." - LinkedIn's own
template, which every reader has seen a thousand times.

## Before writing, ask

1. **The news** - what happened, where, and when.
2. **The story behind it** - one real moment. The rejection before the offer,
   the exam failed the first time, the project that got them promoted, the
   person who helped.
3. **Who to thank** - by name, and only people they genuinely want to thank.
4. **What they want next** - nothing, intros, clients, a job, advice. This
   decides the last line.

## The shape

```
{the news in the first line} + {the one real moment} + {what it taught or changed} + {thanks} + {what is next}
```

- **Lead with the news.** No build-up. "I start at Tarbee on Monday." People
  skim, and the news is why they stopped.
- **One moment, not a career history.** The story is what gets comments.
- **Thank two or three people by name and tag them**, saying what each did.
  A list of twelve names reads as obligation.
- **120 to 250 words.** It is a moment, not an essay.
- **Never** "I'm thrilled to announce", "humbled and honored", "excited to
  embark on a new journey", or "new chapter" as the hook.

## By milestone

- **New job.** What made you say yes, in one honest line. Thank the old team
  without listing grievances. Never criticise the previous employer.
- **Promotion.** Credit the team in a specific sentence. One thing you will do
  differently in the new role.
- **Graduation / certification.** What it actually took - hours, attempts,
  the night it nearly did not happen. Then what you will use it for.
- **Work anniversary.** Only if there is something to say: one thing that
  changed, one thing learned. Otherwise skip it; LinkedIn's default card is
  enough.
- **Leaving a job.** Gracious and short. Say what you are proud of, thank
  people, say what is next or that you are taking time. Nothing that could be
  screenshotted against you.
- **Laid off.** Factual, calm, no blame. Two lines on what happened, three on
  what you do and what you are good at with proof, then one specific ask:
  "If you know a team hiring a backend engineer in Lahore or remote, an intro
  would mean a lot." Specific asks get shared. Hand job-search follow-ups to
  `/li-job`.

## Never

- Never invent a story, a struggle, a number or a thank-you. If the user has
  not given a moment, ask for one, or write a short plain version without it.
- Never name an employer's confidential reasons, layoff numbers or internal
  details the user has not said are public.
- Never post for the user. They paste it and tag people themselves.

## Output

One full draft, one shorter alternative, the people to tag, and a note on
whether to attach a photo (a real one beats the default card). Both drafts go
through `/li-human` first.
